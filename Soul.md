# The Nobel Prince and His Whipping Boy

*To Myself*

## Story Title

The Nobel Prince and His Whipping Boy

## Premise

Naik Singh was born a prince of Banaras but this is not a story about the life he was born into. It is the story of how that life was taken from him, and what he became in its place. Through a series of circumstances driven by politics, power, and the cruelty of circumstance, Naik finds himself bound to the most humiliating role a boy of his blood could occupy: the whipping boy of Prince Louie the Third. When Louie misbehaves, Naik bears the lash. When Louie escapes consequence, Naik absorbs it. The central conflict is not the punishment itself — it is what the punishment reveals about the world both boys have been handed without their consent.

The story is set against colonial-era Banaras, a world already fractured along the lines of race, class, and inherited privilege. Naik moves through that world carrying the double wound of a fallen title and a foreign court's contempt — navigating racism, division, and the specific humiliation of being seen as less than what he knows himself to be. Louie, on the other side of that divide, has never had to question anything the world gave him. But proximity to Naik's reality begins to crack that certainty open. Both boys are kids. Neither has the language yet for what they are living through. But they are learning — from each other, through each other, and sometimes in spite of each other.

At its core this is a story about two children on opposite ends of power slowly dismantling the norms they were raised inside. Naik's journey moves from prince to whipping boy  but the deeper journey is about identity, endurance, and whether dignity can survive what the world does to it. Louie's journey moves from entitlement to awareness a transformation that is uncomfortable, uneven, and honest. Together they question class, race, and privilege not through speeches or grand gestures but through the small, daily, lived reality of what it means to be a boy in a world that has already decided your worth. That tension — intimate, unresolved, deeply human — is what makes this seed rich ground for open collaboration.

## World Rules

- Rule 1: The story is grounded in colonial-era Banaras. No anachronistic technology, language, or social structures. Research before you write.
- Rule 2: Naik Singh's fall from prince to whipping boy is established canon and cannot be retconned. Writers may explore how, but not whether.
- Rule 3: Neither Naik nor Louie may be written as purely good or purely evil. Both must carry contradiction.
- Rule 4: Racism, casteism and class privilege must be portrayed with honesty — not gratuitously, but never sanitized either.
- Rule 5: No character death without a Hearing. Killing a named character requires community approval before it becomes canon.

## Tone & Style Guide

- Tone: Intimate and heavy — the weight of injustice filtered through the eyes of children who don't yet have the words for what they're living through.
- Tense: Past tense.
- POV: Third-person limited. Chapters may follow either Naik or Louie, but not both in the same chapter. State whose POV at the top.
- Voice: Restrained. Let the situation carry the emotion — avoid over-explaining how characters feel. Show the bruise, not the grief.
- Profanity: Period-appropriate language only. No modern slang.
- Sensitivity: Handle race and punishment scenes with care. Graphic violence for shock value will be rejected at Hearing.

## Active Characters

- **Naik Singh**: Former prince of Banaras. Intelligent, quietly proud, learning to survive without losing himself. Bears Louie's punishments without breaking — at least visibly.
- **Prince Louie the Third**: Naught, sheltered, used to consequence landing on others. Not cruel by nature — but untested by it. His transformation is the engine of the story.
- **Raja Chait Singh**: Naik's father. His decisions set the tragedy in motion. His presence — even in absence — haunts Naik throughout.
- **Raja Balwant Singh**: A figure of power in the court. His relationship to both families is deliberately left open for writers to explore.
- **Mahid Singh**: Role and allegiance to be developed through submissions. Seed creators have left this character intentionally open.

## Author Suggestions

These are narrative threads the seed creator proposes for writers to explore. They are not mandatory — but they are fertile ground:

1. **The Indian Maid as Nanny** — A domestic servant who raises Louie alongside Naik, quietly passing down the weight of oppression and racial discrimination through her stories, her silences, and the way she moves through the palace. She knows more than she is permitted to say.
2. **The Racist Noblemen** — The court is full of men who wear their prejudice like a medal. Scenes in the hall, at dinner, during hunts — moments where their casual cruelty reveals the machinery that makes Naik's situation possible and acceptable to everyone around him.
3. **The Classroom** — A toxic environment where Naik and Louie are educated together, and the classroom itself becomes a microcosm of everything wrong with the world outside it. Who gets called on. Who gets punished. What is taught and what is deliberately left out.
